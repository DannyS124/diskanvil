# Code of conduct

DiskAnvil is for anyone who needs to sort out a drive, whatever they know about computers. People often turn up here when something has gone wrong and their files are at stake, so the first rule is simple: be kind and be patient.

## What's expected

- Be friendly and welcoming, to beginners most of all. There are no stupid questions about disks.
- Disagree about the work, not the person. Say what you think and why, and listen when someone sees it differently.
- Give the help you'd want if it were your photos on that drive.
- Keep it on topic: DiskAnvil, drives, and getting PCs going again.

## What's not OK

- Insults, harassment, slurs, or attacks on someone's identity or background
- Sexual content, threats, or anything meant to scare or demean someone
- Posting someone's private details without their permission
- Spam, advertising, or links to malware or pirated software
- Telling people to run commands that would harm their system or their data

## If something goes wrong

If someone is treating you badly, or you see it happening to someone else, tell me privately through [a private report](https://github.com/DannyS124/diskanvil/security/advisories/new). Put "Code of conduct" in the title and say what happened and where. It's only seen by me, and I'll keep who reported it to myself.

You can also use GitHub's own **Report content** option, in the **...** menu on any comment, issue or discussion.

## What happens then

I'll read every report and do what fits: a word with the person, hiding or deleting the comment, locking a thread, or blocking someone from the project. Anyone asked to stop is expected to stop straight away.

This applies everywhere DiskAnvil happens: issues, discussions, pull requests, and anywhere someone speaks for the project.

It's based on the ideas in the [Contributor Covenant](https://www.contributor-covenant.org/), written in plainer words.
